## 1. Question
Can a LoRA adapter learn its own training tasks? G1 is a pipeline check, not a thesis number: it trains and scores on the same 40 tasks, so a pass shows that the pipeline (rows, rendering, training, conversion, serving) can carry learning to task level; a fail sends us to a pipeline-fault search before any fine-tuning conclusion is drawn.

## 3. Adapter and training (`sft/g1_train.py`, new code, 12-micro-batch smoke passed)
* Wide LoRA: q, o, gate, up, down projections on every adapted layer plus k, v where they exist (layers 15 to 34 share KV and have none). **Layers 8 to 34, rank 16, scale 2.0 (alpha 32 in the GGUF), dropout 0**; 149 LoRA modules, 19,759,104 trainable parameters.
* Memory fallbacks used (P0.3, docs in Thesis-wt-r6 `sft/data/distill/r6/p0/p03/*.json`): all 35 layers and layers 8 to 34 both peaked at 48.5 to 49.0 GiB on the longest row (7,547 tokens) on a 48 GiB Mac; layers 8 to 34 with rows above 6,999 tokens dropped peaked at 44.3 GiB (25.8 to 26.2 s per micro-batch at 6,998 tokens). Rank 8 was not needed. This is plan fallbacks 1 and 2 together.
* Optimizer: Adam (as `mlx_lm.lora`), lr 1e-4, linear warm-up over 5% of updates then linear decay to zero, batch 1, gradient accumulation 4, seed 61, prompt mask (loss on the assistant target only), gradient checkpointing, 4 passes with per-pass shuffling, capped at 1,000 micro-batches: 243 x 4 = **972 micro-batches = 243 updates**.
* **Thinking off, in training and in serving.** `mlx_lm.lora` cannot disable thinking (the default template adds a `<|think|>` marker), so rows are rendered with `enable_thinking=False`. Every row is checked: prompt prefix of the full render equals the generation-prompt render, no think marker, BOS exactly once. The served model runs with `--chat-template-kwargs '{"enable_thinking":false}'` (P0.2: 0 markers, no thought channel; `--reasoning-budget 0` was not enough).
* Divergence rule: non-finite loss stops the run (exit 4); if the mean loss of updates 76 to 100 exceeds the mean of updates 1 to 10, it stops (exit 5) and the chain restarts once at lr 5e-5, logged. Training time cap 8 h (exit 6); the chain fails on any non-zero exit, so a cap stop is INVALID, not FAIL.

## 4. Scoring
* Tasks: the 40 tasks above, greedy (sample 0), `distill_student_eval` with `--fc-mode iffc-ooda-card`, 4 workers, stock checker, the same bar GGUF (bartowski Q4_K_M, sha prefix 923c4c86177d2ee1) and llama.cpp build b10894-d344123fe as stage 1.
* Two scored runs, both thinking off: the untuned base, then the adapter (GGUF loaded at runtime, scale 1.0).
* Validity: 40 verdict rows, 40 unique tasks, 0 API-error episodes in both runs, identity checks (model path, build, 4 slots, adapter path and scale) ok, render parity (below) ok. Otherwise the result is INVALID, never PASS or FAIL.
* **Parity gate (before any scoring):** for every one of the 243 rows, the prompt that the server builds (`/apply-template`) equals the prompt the trainer builds (tokenizer, enable_thinking=False) after removing the leading `<bos>` text, and has 0 think markers. Smoke result: 243 of 243 equal, 0 markers (the leading `<bos>` text differs only in that the server adds it at tokenization).
* **Parse rate** is reported next to every pass count: 1 - unparsed outputs / assistant outputs, from the run's decision mix, plus stop share and look-up share.

## 5. Pass rule (as proposed by the advisor and quoted in the approval)
**PASS iff the adapter passes at least 24 of the 40 tasks AND at least 8 more than the untuned base (both thinking off).** Otherwise FAIL (if valid). No other threshold is applied after seeing results.
* Plan stop rule on FAIL, quoted from the plan: "Stop. One day at most to find a pipeline fault (render parity, conversion, scale). If none is found, fine-tuning ends and the write-up is the result." The chain only reports the outcome; acting on a FAIL is the advisor's and Sans's decision, not the chain's.

## 6. Diagnostics (reported, never gating)
* **Row-level in-sample check** (`sft/g1_rowcheck.py`), on the base server and on the adapter server: for each of the 243 training rows, the greedy reply to the row's prompt compared with the row's target after JSON normalisation. Reported: parsed, exact match, exact on stop rows, length-truncated, and tasks with all kept rows exact. These are clues for the fault search, not proof of where a fault lies: low row agreement could come from training, conversion, serving, truncation or imperfect targets; high row agreement with failed task rollouts could come from incomplete row coverage (four tasks lost rows), a difference between single-step replies and multi-turn rollouts, or gold/checker disagreement. A rowcheck failure does not stop the chain.
* Training log: loss per micro-batch (`train_log.jsonl`), peak memory, time.

## 7. Estimate (measured parts marked)
* Training: smoke measured **3.66 s per 1,000 tokens** (12 micro-batches, mean 4,827 tokens, mean 17.7 s each, peak 38.8 GiB). One pass is 1,277,449 tokens, so 972 micro-batches are expected to take about **5.2 h**; if every micro-batch ran as slowly as the longest row (26 s), 7.0 h. Both are below 8 h.
* Scoring and diagnostics: two 40-task scored runs (about 15 min each, scaled from 55 to 60 min per 155 tasks in stage 1) [analysis, unmeasured]; two row checks (243 prompts of about 5k tokens, about 10 min each) [analysis, unmeasured]; two server starts and parity checks (about 2 min each, smoke: under 1 min); conversion and audit (smoke: under 1 min).
* **Whole chain: about 6.3 h expected, 8.3 h worst case; chain cap 10 h** (enforced by the chain). Under the 10 h condition. The first attempt and the registered restart share one 8 h training budget (the chain computes what is left; under 1 h left, no restart and the run is INVALID). A divergence is detected at update 100 (micro-batch 400), about 2.0 h in at the measured rate (4.0 h at the longest-row rate), so a restart adds that much to the chain: about 8.3 h expected with a restart; the worst case with a restart (more than 10 h) would hit the chain cap and be INVALID. Divergence is not expected (the smoke trained 3 updates at the same lr without a problem), so the estimates above are for the no-restart path. Spend $0 (no API). If any measured part runs above the worst case, the chain's 10 h cap fails it as INVALID.

## 8. Conversion audit (before serving)
`gguf-check` compares every adapter matrix pair with the converted GGUF (tolerance 1e-2 on the whole-matrix relative error). The module-name map was extended to all seven module types after reading the real tensor names from a conversion of a random adapter with the exact G1 module set (149 modules, 298 tensors, all present; attn_q, attn_k, attn_v, attn_output, ffn_gate, ffn_up, ffn_down). Smoke on a real 3-update adapter: 149 pairs, alpha 32, error 3.3e-4. The server's `/lora-adapters` reports the adapter path and scale 1.0 (the llama.cpp log prints nothing about adapters; stage 1 already showed that the same mechanism changes outputs, 19 of 70 greedy actions for S0).
