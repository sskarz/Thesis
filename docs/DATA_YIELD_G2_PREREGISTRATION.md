## 1. Question and what a result is
How many checker-verified, accuracy-screened training tasks (and rows) does the teacher pipeline produce at scale, at what cost, and does the Part 1 yield hold when the accepted pool is five times larger? The output feeds G3 (training) only if the acceptance conditions of section 6 hold. It is a yield and quality measurement, not a model result.

## 3. Budget enforcement (not only per-process caps)
1. Preflight: caps arithmetic above; ledger of all earlier timestamped attempts (any response without a cost, or any guard flag raised, in an earlier attempt = refuse).
2. During authoring a monitor sums the cost logs of ALL processes of this attempt every 60 s; above $10.80 (+0 tolerance in dollars, in practice at most one 60 s interval of overshoot, about $0.04 at 3 x 12.5 slots/min [analysis]) or any cost-less response, it terminates all three processes and aborts. Each process also stops itself at its cumulative cap (every chunk gets only the remaining cap).
3. After authoring: judge cap = min($0.50, $15 - A - $2.00), solve cap = min($2.00, $15 - A - judge cap), where A is the measured authoring spend. If the judge cap would be below $0.10 or the solve cap below $0.50, the chain refuses to start them.
4. No automatic extension, top-up or retry that spends money.
   **Incomplete authoring stops the chain before any judge or solve spend:** a process that reaches its own cap before working all its slots (exit 5), a cost-guard stop, a chunk failure, or more than 2% of all slots (206) lost to the deadline. Partial chunks stay on disk; Sans decides.
5. Reporting: `yield_report.py` was changed so its spend and A5 include the judge calls and every earlier timestamped attempt (not only Part 1); Part 1's own report would now include its $0.0128 judge spend.
6. Limit: the ledger counts logged successful responses; requests that the provider billed but that failed or were abandoned at a kill are not visible (up to 3 processes x 4 workers in flight).

## 4. Duplicate risk and logging (pre-registered before any data)
* Part 1 context: `dup_in_batch` was the largest reject reason, 394 of 2,000 slots (19.7%) at 181 accepted tasks; the rule compares with every accepted task, so it can only rise with the pool.
* Per 500 slots and process the chain logs: accepted, `dup_in_batch`, gold errors, other rejects, deadline, API rejects, spend (`progress.jsonl`, printed at the end of authoring, shown live by the monitor).
* Halfway projection (written once, as soon as the three processes together have worked 5,150 slots): `projected survivors = valid tasks so far x 0.392 x (10,300 / slots worked)`, compared with N = 400 and the floor 250, saved as `author/halfway_projection.json` and printed with a flag. It is a projection from one earlier run, not a result. If it is below the floor the flag says so and **Sans decides on a top-up; the chain never extends itself**.
* Cross-process duplicates are removed after authoring by `yield_merge.py` (same 8-word n-gram rule, first process wins, every drop listed).

## 5. Steps (all CPU/API; no GPU, no port 8080; processes at `nice 10`). Two phases
**Phase 1 (`g2_chain.sh full`)** ends after the pre-solve filter and writes `PHASE 1 COMPLETE`. **Phase 2 (`g2_chain.sh solve ATTEMPT_DIR`)** is the solve and everything after it; it refuses to run until the amendment in section 9 is implemented, reviewed and `SOLVE_SENTENCES_READY=1` is set by that commit.

smoke (3 x 4 slots; zero API rejects; every response with a cost; pinned model and provider) -> authoring -> aggregate -> merge/dedupe -> gold audit -> deterministic state checks -> request-coverage judge (calibrated: drops 6 of 7 wrong and 0 of 27 faithful on the 40 reviewed tasks) -> pre-solve filter (judge FAITHFUL and no hard state flag) -> teacher solve under the compact-list stack -> held-out filter -> JSON-only rows -> conflict dedupe -> provenance gate -> `yield_report.py` -> `g2_tools floor` (writes `G2_REPORT.json`) -> 40-task review sample.
**Design change against Part 1:** the judge and state checks run BEFORE the solve (so no paid solve is spent on screened-out tasks). Consequence: the solve input, called `audited.jsonl` in this attempt for `yield_report.py`, is the screened set; the full audited set is `audited_all.jsonl`; `yield_report.py` funnel ratios that divide by "audited" are therefore not comparable with Part 1's. The survivors-per-slot figure is comparable.

## 6. Acceptance and stop rules
* Reported as in Part 1 (E0, A2 to A6, provenance), plus: **floor_met** = at least 250 surviving tasks AND at least 1,500 clean rows (the plan's stop rule); **target_met** = at least 400 surviving tasks.
* Surviving task = in `filtered/tasks_F.jsonl` (teacher-verified, held-out filter) after the judge and state screens.
* A1 (accuracy): fresh 40-task sample (seed 9092) of surviving tasks, reviewed by Codex (labeling mode) and two blind subagents; at most 2 WRONG per reviewer; the wording of a pass stays "no evidence of a high error rate".
* A6 (at most 2% of slots lost to the deadline or API failures): not loosened after the fact. In this run the chain additionally stops before the judge and solve when more than 2% of slots are lost (see section 3), so a run that would fail A6 on the deadline does not spend the downstream money.
* If floor_met is false the result is reported as a yield shortfall; no further authoring is started without a `SANS APPROVED` line.

## 7. Time estimate (parts marked)
Authoring: 275 min expected at the Part 1 rate per process if the API scales to 3 processes (unmeasured; if the three processes share a rate limit the 343 min deadline cuts the run and A6 fails); hard stop 343 min. After authoring [analysis, unmeasured at this scale except where noted]: merge a few minutes; audit and state checks (CPU, runs every gold) 20 to 40 min; judge about 10 min (Part 1: 56 s for 99 tasks, measured); solve about 1 h (Part 1: 724 episodes in 14.1 min, measured; here about 2,900 episodes); filter, rows, dedupe about 10 min; provenance up to 1 h (Part 1: minutes, five times the rows); report and sample minutes. **Whole run about 7.5 h expected, chain cap 12.7 h (343 + 420 min).** Spend expected about $11, cap $15.

## 8. Limits stated up front
The 3.9% survivor rate rests on one run (71 survivors; Wilson 95% interval about 3.1% to 4.9% of attempted slots [analysis]) and does not include the duplicate saturation described in section 4; 400 survivors is therefore an estimate that could be low or high. The rate limit of the provider at 12 concurrent requests is untested. The judge was calibrated on 40 tasks and its error classes were informed by those tasks. Teacher and judge are the same model family. The held-out filter and the provenance gate protect the 200 BFCL tasks, the 20-task gate, F, K2 and the other held-out banks only to the extent of the Round 5 definitions. Tasks come from the same pipeline as the F and K2 screening banks; FK cannot settle a claim about the BFCL 200.

## 9. Phase 2: the teacher solve with reasoning sentences (specification; Codex-reviewed before it runs)
Run as `bash sft/g2_phase2.sh ATTEMPT_DIR` from worktree `Thesis-wt-g2p2` (branch `thesis/g2-phase2`), because the phase 1 worktree must not change while phase 1 runs. (The `solve` mode of `g2_chain.sh` stays locked and is superseded by this script.) It refuses to run on an attempt that is not a finished phase 1, that already has a phase 2, or while any authoring/judge/solve process is running; it checks the K2 and F hashes, that `benchmark/` is identical to the slim-port worktree, and the ledger of the attempt and of all earlier attempts (a response without a cost, or a raised guard flag, stops it).
* **Mode and settings:** `iffc-state-ooda-slim`, k = 4, 4 workers, `--max-cost` = min(4.00, 15 - spent so far in this run), at least 0.50 must remain, whole phase 2 under an 8 h cap, solve at most 180 min. Why 4.00 and not the 2.00 of the phase 1 table: replies now carry two or three sentences, so more output tokens per call; Part 1's solve cost $0.215 for 724 episodes JSON-only (measured), the sentences are expected to raise that several-fold, not to the cap.
* **Sentence instruction:** appended in `g2_sentences.SentenceClient._body` to a copy of the last user message in the HTTP request body only. The harness and the stock client log the unchanged slim prompts (`messages` of every call and therefore every row prompt contain no instruction; unit test `test_g2_sentences.py`).
* **Parsing:** the call list begins at the first line-initial `[` or `{` from which the remainder parses as a call list; text before it is the sentences. If no clean line-initial list exists, the call list is re-serialised from what the stock parser read and the episode has no sentences. The harness (history, checker, later turns) only ever sees the call list, so verdicts and arm A rows are produced by the same path as Part 1.
* **Sentence validity (arm B only):** 2 or 3 sentences, each at most 700 characters, no braces, no phrase from the leak list (`ground truth`, `gold`, `checker`, `grader`, `expected call/answer/result`, `the correct call/answer/action`, `reference answer`, `hint`, `private`, `instruction`, `as instructed`, `reviewer`, `note`, `the task says/requires/expects`). A row whose sentences fail stays in arm A and is excluded from arm B; nothing is edited. The regex is a floor: before any arm B training, 40 sentence sets are read by Codex and two subagents for answer-naming (not part of phase 2's automatic output).
* **Arms:** arm A = the rows exactly as in Part 1 (JSON call list). Arm B = the same rows, joined by (episode id, hash of the prompt messages, hash of the call list), with the sentences as `reasoning_content` of the final assistant turn and the same call list as content. The share of arm A rows that reach arm B, and the stop-row (empty list) share of each, are reported.
* **Floors (on arm A train rows, not tasks alone):** fewer than 250 surviving tasks or fewer than 1,500 arm A train rows = stop and report. Arm B reports its own train row count against the same 1,500. No top-up or extension runs without a SANS APPROVED line.
* **Provenance:** the gate runs on arm A and on arm B. `check_provenance.py` now also checks every `reasoning_content` against the BFCL exclusion list (excluded task ids, 8-grams of excluded user turns, exact excluded turns) and fails if any row hits; tested on a doctored row (a BFCL id in the reasoning fails, a clean 15-row sample passes).
* **Review round 1 of this phase** (`docs/reviews/codex_g2d_phase2.md`) found two blockers, both fixed: prose in a same-line reply reached the log (now the content is always the call list only) and BFCL text in reasoning was not checked (above).

## 11. Registered deviation: deterministic repair of the id_collision defect (advisor note 2026-11-10 09:36 ET; written before any repaired task is used)
* **Defect.** 134 of the 734 merged tasks (139 flags; 133 with no other hard flag) have a backend counter (TwitterAPI.tweet_counter or TradingBot.order_counter) at or below an id already in the starting state, so the first object created by the gold overwrites an existing one. It is one mechanical defect, not a wrong request.
* **Repair** (`sft/yield_repair.py`, CPU only, no API): set that counter to (largest id in that backend table) + 1. Nothing else changes; gold calls and user turns are byte-identical. A task is kept only if all hard state checks pass again and no gold call errors. Class R1 = every non-creation gold call returns exactly what it returned before; class R2 = at least one such call returns something different (the collision had changed what a later call saw), so its gold results change although calls and turns do not.
* **Result** (attempt 20261109_212657_g2full.AUhok0, `repair/repair_report.json`): 134 id_collision tasks; 129 kept (R1 107, R2 22); dropped 4 (error result after repair) and 1 (empty_read_with_state_key after repair).
* **Gate before any repaired task joins the pool.** Seeded sample of 20 repaired tasks, seed 9093, 14 R1 + 6 R2 (R2 oversampled because it is the risky class), reviewed by Codex with the same A1 labelling prompt as before. Rule: at most 2 WRONG of 20, and at most 1 WRONG among the 6 R2. If R2 fails and R1 passes, only R1 joins; if R1 fails, none. Repaired tasks that join go through the judge, the solve and the held-out filter like all others and are listed in the report (ids and class); the unrepaired versions of those tasks are not used.
